Existing Rotation: Continuous Corn–Soybean (2-year)

Baseline

Why this is common

High market demand, established equipment, strong short-term returns, and familiarity. Soybean provides some nitrogen credit for the following corn.

Supporting patterns

  • MODIS NDVI shows strong peak biomass in both crops under good conditions.
  • NASA POWER precipitation supports adequate water in average years for both crops.

Benefits

  • Simple management and marketing.
  • Relatively high short-term profitability in good years.

Limitations observed

  • Long periods of bare soil after harvest → higher erosion risk during intense rainfall (GPM extremes).
  • Build-up of soybean cyst nematode and other pests with short rotations.
  • Limited organic matter accumulation and poorer infiltration compared with more diverse systems.
  • Higher fertilizer N needs for corn phase.

Information gaps & risks

Local soil tests, nematode pressure, and exact market contracts are essential. Extreme weather years can still cause large yield swings. This tool does not replace on-farm trials or advisor recommendations.

Diversified Rotation: Corn – Soybean – Winter Wheat / Oats + Cover Crops

Recommended for Resilience

Why suggested

Adds a small grain to break pest cycles, keep living roots longer, reduce bare-soil windows, and improve soil structure. Research across the Midwest shows yield stability gains and reduced nitrate leaching.

Evidence support

  • Longer living-root periods improve infiltration (beneficial under high-intensity precipitation events seen in GPM data).
  • Small grains can utilize residual N and reduce tile nitrate losses.
  • MODIS/ECOSTRESS patterns suggest moderated heat stress with better soil cover.
  • University trials (Minnesota, Illinois, Nebraska, Ohio) document improved soil health indicators and corn yield stability.

Potential benefits

  • Interrupts pest & disease cycles (e.g., SCN).
  • Better erosion control and organic matter trend.
  • Possible double-crop or forage opportunity after wheat.
  • Improved water infiltration and drought buffering.

Information gaps

Local markets for wheat/oats, equipment availability for small grains, and precise cover-crop termination timing need farmer/expert input. Yield of the third crop varies with weather.

Risks requiring local review

  • Learning curve and possible short-term yield dip while system adjusts.
  • Herbicide carryover constraints.
  • Market price volatility for small grains.

This is a decision-support suggestion only. Validate with local Extension and on-farm experimentation.

Soil-Building Rotation: Corn – Soybean – Alfalfa / Mixed Forage (or Oats + Legume)

Strong Soil Health Focus

Why suggested

Perennial or multi-year forages add deep roots, continuous cover, biological nitrogen fixation, and significant organic matter. Long-term Ohio and Nebraska studies show higher and more stable corn yields after forage phases, especially under no-till.

Evidence support

  • Deep roots improve soil structure and water holding capacity — valuable under variable SMAP soil-moisture regimes.
  • Nitrogen fixation reduces synthetic N needs for subsequent corn.
  • Near-continuous cover dramatically cuts erosion during extreme precipitation.
  • Improved aggregate stability and microbial activity reported in long-term trials.

Potential benefits

  • Highest potential soil organic matter gains.
  • Supplemental forage income or livestock integration.
  • Strongest resilience to drought and intense rain.
  • Breaks pest cycles most effectively.

Information gaps

Requires livestock or reliable forage markets. Establishment cost and multi-year commitment. Local soil pH, drainage, and weed pressure must be assessed.

Risks requiring local review

  • Cash-flow timing (forage years vs. grain years).
  • Termination and transition back to annuals needs careful planning.
  • Not suitable for every farm business model.

Best suited where livestock or hay markets exist, or where long-term soil capital is a priority. Always consult local experts.

  • Your actual soil test results and drainage
  • Landlord or bank requirements
  • Equipment you already own (or can rent)
  • Local markets and delivery contracts

About This Tool & Data Sources

NASA Datasets Referenced

  • SMAP – Soil moisture
  • GPM – Precipitation extremes
  • MODIS / VIIRS – Vegetation (NDVI), true color, land surface temperature
  • ECOSTRESS – Evapotranspiration & heat stress concepts
  • NASA POWER – Temperature, precipitation, humidity (live API used)
  • GRACE / GRACE-FO – Regional water storage trends
  • SRTM – Elevation context

How to Adapt

Change the latitude/longitude in the JavaScript for any farm. Update the GIBS date and layer for recent imagery. The NASA POWER fetch can be pointed at any coordinates. Local soil surveys (SSURGO), yield history, and farmer priorities should be layered on top.

Intended Use

Supportive exploration of options that may improve soil health and climate resilience. Final decisions remain with the farmer and local experts.
